Case 01 / Research workflow

Turning prior-art research into a reviewable workflow

I wanted the speed of AI-assisted research without asking a reviewer to trust a polished answer they could not trace. The answer was to redesign the work around evidence and checkpoints, not around one long prompt.

My roleAI lead / workflow designer
DomainPatent analysis and reverse engineering
Working materialsStructured workbook, LLMs, Python-assisted research
Main constraintEvery material conclusion had to be reviewable by a person
The problem

A good answer was not enough. The reviewer needed to see how the answer was built.

The first thing I changed was the one-big-prompt approach.

Prior-art work is really several jobs tied together. You have to lock the claim scope, break the claim into elements, search patent and non-patent sources, map evidence to each element, prove that the source predates the priority date, and then make a judgment about how strong the record actually is.

If all of that happens inside one free-form model response, the result can look complete even when one of those steps is weak. I separated the work into stages and gave each stage a fixed output. That made missing evidence easier to spot and made review much faster to reason about.

What I was optimizing for

A reviewer should be able to move from recommendation to claim element to quote to source to publication date without trusting the model's memory.

The workflow

Each step has a job, a defined output, and a reason to stop if the record is not ready.

I turned the research into six visible steps.

01
Lock the scope
Patent, claims, priority date, search scope, and the expected endpoint.
02
Break the claim down
Create labeled elements while preserving the exact claim language in a separate field.
03
Map the evidence
For each element, capture the source, exact location, short supporting quote, support type, and notes.
04
Prove the date
Every material reference gets its own date-proof record showing that it predates the priority date.
05
Make the call
Record the recommendation, confidence, basis, unresolved gaps, and what still needs to happen.
06
Strengthen and review
Add independent support, tighten the citations, narrow the gaps, and hand the package to the human reviewer.
One important decision

The original source and my interpretation never live in the same field.

Keep the evidence harder to distort than the explanation.

I kept verbatim claim text separate from the plain-language gloss. I did the same thing with prior art: the source, pin, and short quote stay distinct from the note explaining why the evidence matters.

That seems small, but it changes the review experience. A reviewer can disagree with an interpretation without first having to reconstruct the underlying evidence.

Decision behavior

I do not want a low-confidence result to move through the workflow the same way a strong one does.

When the evidence changes, the next action changes too.

Confidence is useful only if it affects behavior. Strong support can move forward for human review. Mixed support should stop and name the blocking gap. Weak or unverified support should return control instead of trying to write around the problem.

What I can support

Next step

Advance the package to human review with a concise basis.

Reflection

The fixed structure solved one problem and created the next design question.

What worked, and what I would change next.

What workedThe same inputs, fields, and review gates made the work more consistent from matter to matter.
What stayed humanInterpretation, legal significance, and the final professional judgment stayed outside the model's authority.
What I would build nextA reviewer view that drills from recommendation to element to source, records overrides, and makes evidence gaps visible without opening several files.

The larger lesson

For me, the interesting design problem was not how to get a smarter answer. It was how to make the system easier to question. That is the pattern I would carry into other agent-assisted products.
