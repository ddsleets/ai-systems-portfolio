EVOLVE RF / Physical-World Technical Proof

Distributed RF observability built around evidence, uncertainty, and cooperative sensing.

EVOLVE RF asks a harder version of the EVOLVE question: how do multiple imperfect physical-world observations become one useful, auditable event without hiding timing quality, measurement limits, uncertainty, or human authority? The public proof shows the system behavior and evidence boundaries without exposing the private RF Core.

Evidence boundary

This public proof separates tested software behavior, reasoning-evaluation evidence, and physical RF validation. It does not expose proprietary source, private thresholds, unpublished IP/FTO material, credentials, customer data, or live telemetry. Synthetic and architecture-level examples are labeled as such.

The problem

Useful RF evidence is often scattered across devices, measurement types, clock qualities, and locations.

One observation is not the same thing as one RF event.

EVOLVE separates device-level observations from the higher-level event being investigated. Multiple sensors may see the same RF behavior with different timing quality, signal fidelity, location context, and measurement capability.

Core rule: a device owns an observation. EVOLVE owns the event.

The architecture preserves each observation's provenance, qualifies what each node can actually measure, and combines compatible evidence without pretending all sensors are equivalent.

System flow

Edge observations become persistent correlated events only after capability, timing, and provenance checks.

AI assists interpretation. It does not get to rewrite physics.

01
Observe
A device reports a capability-bounded observation with timing, signal, and provenance information.
02
Detect
Local observations are compared with adaptive baselines and grouped into local events when warranted. Learned or model-assisted detection/classification may operate here when evidence supports it.
03
Correlate
Compatible observations from multiple devices are associated into a persistent cross-device RF event, with source identity and event history preserved.
04
Locate carefully
RSSI, TDoA, AoA, or hybrid methods are considered only when node capabilities and timing quality justify them. Readiness gates and uncertainty remain deterministic evidence constraints.
05
Cooperate
A fusion service may request additional sensing from eligible edge nodes, with command provenance and human/policy authority preserved.
Architecture boundaryModel assistance is not decision authority
Model-assisted / learned

Recognition, prioritization, and evidence synthesis.

AI/RFML can help detect or classify signal behavior, rank candidate explanations, summarize multi-node evidence, and support an operator's understanding of an event. Its outputs remain evidence to be evaluated—not permission to act.

Deterministic / physics-constrained

Readiness gates, timing validity, localization constraints, and authority.

Clock qualification, node capability, TDoA/AoA readiness, provenance, uncertainty requirements, command authorization, and mitigation permission are enforced independently of model confidence.

A model can help explain what the evidence may mean. It cannot make an unavailable measurement valid, manufacture clock quality, or grant itself operational authority.
The software treats clock quality and measurement capability as part of the evidence.

Scope boundary

These results support reasoning-quality and guardrail claims only. They do not establish physical RF detection, classification, localization, or field performance.

What is not established

The current proof is explicit about what still requires physical-world validation.

Software and reasoning readiness are not the same thing as RF field validation.

Device accessProduction access to specific commercial phones, routers, modems, access points, SDRs, chipsets, drivers, or firmware remains device- and platform-dependent.
Detection / classificationField sensitivity, false-alarm rate, classification accuracy, and calibrated cross-vendor measurement equivalence require representative hardware testing.
LocalizationReal-world emitter-location accuracy and validated TDoA/AoA solver performance require controlled physical trials.
Production operationsSecurity certification, high-availability scale, fleet behavior, and any autonomous mitigation authority require separate production validation and governance.
