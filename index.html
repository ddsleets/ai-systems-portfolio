Systems design for AI workflows

I came to AI design through engineering.

I am not a career product designer. I spent most of my career building, testing, troubleshooting, and leading complex technical systems. More recently I brought that same discipline into AI-assisted engineering and intellectual-property work. I start with the real workflow, make the decisions and failure states visible, prototype what is uncertain, and keep a person in control when judgment matters.

How engineering shaped me

The model is only one part of the system.

Years of integration, test, and field work changed how I approach AI. I assume the environment will change, inputs will be incomplete, tools will fail, and users will sometimes disagree with the system. I would rather design for those moments up front.

01. Start with the decisionWhat is the person actually trying to decide or finish?
02. Make state visibleThe user should know what the system is doing and what it touched.
03. Verify before advancingA plausible result is not the same thing as a confirmed one.
04. Keep judgment with the userWhen consequence rises, the handoff should get clearer, not quieter.
05. Test the ugly statesTimeouts, missing context, conflicting evidence, and recovery are part of the product.
How I lead the work

I stay close enough to the work to know when the process is getting in the way.

Most of my leadership roles have put me between people with different priorities: engineers, software teams, security, operations, clients, counsel, and senior leaders. I translate the problem, write down the decision criteria, surface tradeoffs early, and make ownership and next steps explicit.

I am comfortable giving direct technical feedback, running reviews, teaching a new method, and then getting back into the details myself when the hard part is not resolved yet.

A product question I want to explore

What should an agent look like when it is working beside a team, not behind a chat box?

In a shared canvas, I would not want agent status hidden in a spinner or buried in a transcript. I would want the work itself to show what the agent is doing, what changed, what it needs from the user, and whether the action can be reversed.

This is a concept, not shipped work. I would prototype the behavior quickly, put it in front of real teams, and expect the interaction to change once people use it.
