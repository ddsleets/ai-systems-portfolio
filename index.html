How I run technical programs

Go deep enough to make the decision better.

I do not separate program management from technical judgment. I want the requirements, architecture, test evidence, failure modes, risks, owners, and next decision visible enough that the team can move quickly without pretending uncertainty has disappeared.

01. Define the decisionStart with the mission and the evidence required to advance, not a feature list.
02. Get technically deepUnderstand the engineering well enough to challenge assumptions, test design, and release claims.
03. Structure ambiguityTurn open problems into workstreams, gates, owners, dependencies, and measurable next steps.
04. Make failure visibleDomain drift, bad provenance, unsafe authority, and model disagreement become program inputs, not hidden exceptions.
05. Tie evidence to readinessState what can proceed, what needs revision, what must halt, and what requires more information.
Technical leadership

I stay close enough to the engineering to know when the program story is getting ahead of the evidence.

Most of my career has put me between people with different priorities: RF engineers, software teams, systems teams, security, operations, customers, counsel, and senior leaders. I translate the problem, establish the decision criteria, surface tradeoffs early, and keep ownership and next actions explicit.

The AI/RFML work extends that operating model into model evaluation and deployment readiness: strong technical results should move the program forward, but they should never erase a known limitation or an unresolved safety, authority, or evidence-integrity issue.
